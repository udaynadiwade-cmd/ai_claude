Airbus expects to deliver roughly 120 aircraft a year into India for the next
decade. Two a week, for ten years.

Everyone is counting the aircraft. Almost nobody is counting the hangars.

Every one of those aircraft lands into a maintenance schedule on day one. A
checks, C checks, line maintenance at every station it touches. And every
hangar that takes it on needs the same unglamorous things ready before the
first tow-in: ground power, tugs and towbars, stairs and docking, jacks,
tooling calibrated and tagged, and the consumables that run out on a Friday
night with an aircraft due out Saturday morning.

I spent 1999 to 2003 at GE Aircraft Engines sourcing MRO parts across Asia,
China first, for engine shops in the US and Europe. I know the hangar side of
this business from the supply end. The certified part is the visible cost.
The kit around it is where the delays live.

So, plainly, where Befach fits and where it does not.

We do not supply certified flight hardware. Anything that goes on the
aircraft needs an airworthiness release and an approval chain we are not part
of, and I know that chain too well to pretend otherwise.

What we do is the hangar around the part. Ground support equipment. Hangar
and line tooling. Shop consumables, safety kit, storage and handling. Sourced
from China and across Asia, on befach.com with the landed price on the page,
duties and freight included, customs cleared to your door.

The order book is twelve years long. Hangar capacity is a twelve-week
problem, and it is the one that grounds aircraft on a Saturday.

If you run an engineering base or an MRO in India: search the kit on
befach.com, read the landed price, order it. No RFQ, no quote to chase.
