The global commercial aircraft backlog crossed 17,000 this year. Seventeen
thousand and thirty-seven, as of July. At today's build rates that is more than
twelve years of production already spoken for.

Airbus alone is carrying a backlog of over 1,200 aircraft for India, and
expects to hand over roughly 120 a year for the next decade. Two a week.

Everyone reads that as an order book. I read it as a procurement problem.

I spent 1999 to 2003 inside that industry, as Country Sourcing Leader at GE
Aircraft Engines. My job was finding and qualifying suppliers for an engine
maker. The lesson that stayed with me was not about price. It was that the
approval chain is the product. A part is not a part until the paperwork says
it is, and no supplier gets a shortcut through that, however good the quote.

That is why I read this backlog differently.

A backlog that long does not get cleared by
the people who assemble aircraft. It gets cleared by the tier twos and tier
threes who have to double capacity on a deadline set by somebody else. And
capacity is not an order book. It is machines, tooling and inspection kit
standing on a floor in Bengaluru or Hyderabad, commissioned and running.

That is a sourcing problem, and it is the one I spent twenty years on.

So, plainly, where Befach fits and where it does not.

We do not touch certified flight hardware. Anything that flies needs
airworthiness release, traceability and an approval chain we are not part of,
and in this industry there is no clever way around that. Anyone offering you a
shortcut there is selling you a future AOG and a regulator's letter.

What we do is everything behind it. Cutting tools and tool holders. Fixtures
and jigs. Metrology and test equipment. Cleanroom and shop-floor consumables.
Material handling. The unglamorous list that decides whether a line is
commissioned in March or in September, landed-cost visible before you commit.

The backlog is twelve years long. The equipment to attack it is a twelve-week
lead time problem, and that is where it is usually lost.

If you are scaling an aerospace line in India and your bottleneck is kit rather
than orders, that conversation I would genuinely enjoy.
