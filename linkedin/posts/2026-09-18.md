Airbus expects to deliver roughly 120 aircraft a year into India for the next
decade. Two a week, for ten years.

Everyone is counting the aircraft. Almost nobody is counting the hangars.

Every one of those aircraft lands into a maintenance schedule on day one. A
checks, C checks, line maintenance at every station it touches. And every
hangar that takes it on needs the same unglamorous things ready before the
first tow-in: ground power, tugs and towbars, stairs and docking, jacks,
tooling calibrated and tagged, and the consumables that run out on a Friday
night with an aircraft due out Saturday morning.

I spent 1999 to 2003 at GE Aircraft Engines sourcing MRO parts across Asia,
China first, for engine shops in the US and Europe. I know the hangar side of
this business from the supply end. The aircraft is the visible cost. The
hangar around it is where the delays live.

And it is not only hangars. Every plant feeding this backlog has a maintenance
store with the same Friday problem, the same purchase order stuck at the same
desk, for the same kind of kit.

Where Befach comes in is exactly that kit. Ground support equipment. Hangar,
line and shop-floor tooling. Consumables, safety kit, storage and handling.
Sourced from China and across Asia, on befach.com with the landed price on
the page, duties and freight included, customs cleared to your door.

You do not need an account manager, a call, or a quote. Search it, read the
landed price, order it. It arrives like anything else you buy online, with a
GST invoice.

The order book is twelve years long. The kit that keeps it moving is a
twelve-week problem, and it is the one that grounds an aircraft on a Saturday
or idles a line on a Monday.
