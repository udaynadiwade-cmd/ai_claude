I was at Guwahati airport a couple of years ago.

Someone sent me a walkthrough of the new terminal this morning.

The roof is a bamboo forest. Not bamboo-themed. A forest, in timber, holding up
a terminal.

The detail that stopped me was about the architect, Nuru Karim. As the video
tells it, he never trained as an architect. He studied mathematics, and only
later worked out that mathematics and creativity were not two different
departments.

I have spent a career being told people like me are the opposite of creative.
Supply chain. Six Sigma. Landed cost to two decimals. The assumption is that if
you think in numbers, someone else does the imagining.

That roof is 140 tonnes of wood, according to the video, spanning a terminal
built for 13 million passengers a year. You do not get to that with taste. You
get there with structural maths so sound that it can afford to be beautiful.

The other half is that none of it is decoration. The bamboo is Assam's bholuka.
The structure nods to the Apatani community's building tradition. The planting
is the foxtail orchid. The central space is shaped around Kamakhya, where
people come to pray to the divine feminine. Every choice is a local fact, not a
mood board.

Her closing line is the one I keep turning over:

Good architecture helps you feel where you are, instead of telling you.

Which is a decent test for anything you build. A product, a catalogue, a
company. Does it tell people what it is, or does it let them feel it.

Most of what I have built told people. I am still learning the other thing.
