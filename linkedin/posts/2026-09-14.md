Five listings on our catalog this week. All of them called AI smart glasses.

Cheapest: ₹354 a piece. Dearest: ₹6,028 a unit.

Seventeen times, inside one product name.

That spread is the whole reason importing eats first-timers. The category name
carries no information. You are not comparing products, you are comparing a
word.

Then the second trap, which is worse, because the first one at least looks
suspicious.

Here is the actual sheet on the ₹6,028 unit, door delivered to India:

Factory price: ₹6,028
Duties, taxes, freight to the door: ₹4,417
Total: ₹10,445

The procurement line is 73% on top of the factory price. The number everybody
negotiates is 58% of what they actually pay.

And it does not scale with price. It scales with weight and lane. That unit is
0.8 kg. One of the alternatives weighs 0.142 kg. Same shelf, same search
result, completely different economics once it moves.

Which means the ₹354 piece is not the cheap one. It is the one where freight
and duty are several times the product. You find that out after you have paid.

This is why every listing carries a landed price and not a China price. Not as
a feature. Because a China price is not an answer to any question a buyer
actually has.

If you import: do you know your landed cost per unit on your top SKU, or do you
know your supplier's quote?
