## 1. Name and philosophy — CONFIRMED

**Befach** is an acronym: **Be**tter, **Fa**ster, **Ch**eaper.

The **4X** in the company name is the size of the delta. Every product or offering
should aim to be roughly **four times** better, faster and cheaper than the existing
market alternative — four, because the gap has to be large enough that *customers
come to Befach rather than Befach chasing customers*.

This is not only an external product standard. It applies to how the office works:
every process, tool and method is judged on whether it moves productivity or
customer satisfaction by ~4x. A tool that gives 10% is not interesting.

**Implication for anyone working here, agents included:** proposing an incremental
improvement is off-standard. If a task can only be improved 10%, say so and go look
for the thing that can be improved 4x.

## 4. Origin story — CONFIRMED

**2018 — D'Cal water softener.** Started with no capital and no clear plan; came out
of home experimentation. Gained traction on Amazon, then through distributors,
dealers and offline sales nationally. Honest assessment: not a strong-quality
product, recurring customer issues, and it could not be built into a proper brand
because there was no R&D background or investment behind it.

**~2020 — Befach Rice.** Low-GI, diabetic-friendly rice. Added the same way, without
a structured business-building process. Could not be expanded into a full brand for
lack of food-industry background and focus.

**2023 — the deliberate move.** Reserves from D'Cal and Befach Rice funded a
considered entry into **import simplification for India** — chosen because it matches
Uday's supply chain / sourcing / logistics background and both founders' preference
for sourcing and distribution over manufacturing.

**Validation.** Tested with a single Meta ad, no website, offering end-to-end import
services. Generated **~100 clients** — mostly one-off sample orders, with **one client
repeating 5–6 times**. Built real expertise in customs, logistics and China sourcing.
Team scaled to ~30 people across tech, sourcing, research and marketing.

**2023–2025 — the stall.** Could not operationalise smoothly: no proper website,
inconsistent process, no Alibaba-style catalog. Growth flattened.

**Last 6–8 months — the unlock.** AI tooling made catalog-building tractable. Befach
now has **API access to Alibaba and 1688.com**, can pull from other sourcing and
dropshipping platforms, calculates landed price systematically, and has the catalog
**live at befach.com**. Part of the tech team was recently replaced with more AI-savvy
hires.

### The pattern worth naming — ASSUMED

Two of three product lines stalled for the same reason: entering a category where
neither founder had domain depth, without a structured build process. The imports
business is the first one chosen *because* it matches founder capability. That is the
reason to expect a different outcome — and the reason any proposal to diversify into
a new category should have to answer "which founder has depth here?" first.

**Mission:** simplify international trade for India, starting with imports — making

## 7. Open flag — landed price method

The catalog's landed price is described as **China base price + customs duty +
shipping + margin**.

Read literally, that sequence computes duty **before** freight is added. Indian
customs assesses duty on the **CIF assessable value** — FOB *plus* freight *plus*
insurance — and IGST is then charged on top of (AV + BCD + SWS + any AIDC/ADD). If
the catalog computes duty on the China base price alone, every quoted landed price is
understated, and understated systematically across the whole catalog.

This may simply be shorthand for a formula that is already correct. It is worth
checking once against the assessment order in `sourcing/reference/landed-cost.md`,
using `sourcing/tools/landed_cost.py`, which implements the customs sequence and
shows the line-by-line cascade. At catalog scale a structural error here is a margin
leak on every order, not a rounding difference.
