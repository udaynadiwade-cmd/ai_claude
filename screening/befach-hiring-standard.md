# The Befach Hiring Standard

**This is the constant. Job descriptions change; this does not.**

Set by Uday, 2026-08-07. Applies to every role Befach hires for, present and
future. Role-specific rubrics slot *into* this frame — they never replace it.

Befach is a startup. That is not a detail, it is the whole filter. We are not
hiring people to occupy a seat and execute a defined scope. We are hiring people
who behave like owners of the problem in front of them.

---

## The three pillars

| Pillar | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Attitude** | 35 | The thing that cannot be taught |
| **Aptitude** | 35 | Role-specific skill + relevant education + raw thinking |
| **Communication** | 30 | Clarity of thought, and understanding priorities |

**Years of experience is explicitly NOT a pillar.** Uday's words: *"experience is
not a very big factor."* Do not score it. Score what they have *done* — projects,
assignments, things they built, things they tried that failed. A candidate with
two years and four self-started projects outranks one with six years of
maintaining someone else's process.

---

## 1. ATTITUDE — 35 points

The bar is **8–9 out of 10**. That is deliberately high. We would rather leave a
seat empty than fill it with a 6.

### What we are looking for

**Founder mentality — 12 pts.** Wants to get it done. Takes the problem as theirs.
Doesn't wait to be told the next step, doesn't hand a half-finished thing back.

**Tried, failed, tried again — 10 pts.** Evidence of iteration. Someone who has
started things, had them not work, and started again. Failure is not a mark
against a candidate here — *absence of attempts* is.

**Solution-oriented, not problem-oriented — 8 pts.** Brings answers, not a
running list of blockers. The difference between "this won't work because X" and
"X is in the way, here are two ways round it."

**Openness and range — 5 pts.** Whatever they are handed, they take it on. No
"that's not my job."

### How to score this from a CV — and its limits

Attitude is the hardest thing to read on paper. **Be honest about that.** The CV
stage can reliably screen *out* obvious low attitude; it cannot confirm high
attitude. Score provisionally, mark confidence low, and let the interview decide.

| Positive signal | Negative signal |
|---|---|
| Self-started projects, side ventures, freelance work with real output | Only ever executed a defined scope under supervision |
| Startup or early-stage experience | Purely large-company process roles with no ownership |
| Things they built that didn't work — and what came next | A CV with no evidence of anything attempted independently |
| Range: took on things outside their title | Narrow, repetitive, unchanged responsibilities year on year |
| Application itself is thoughtful and specific | Mass-mailed generic application |

**Interview is where this is actually decided.** Ask: *"Tell me about something
you started that failed. What did you do next?"* The pause before the answer
tells you most of it.

## 2. APTITUDE — 35 points

Two parts: what the role specifically needs, and whether they can think.

**Role-specific skill — 20 pts.** Drawn from that role's own rubric. Examples of
what this means per function:

| Function | Aptitude means |
|---|---|
| Digital / performance marketing | Running ads on Google, Meta, YouTube; measurement; GA4 |
| Social media | How every platform actually works, natively |
| Automation | Automation tools, and process design |
| Sourcing & logistics | How global logistics actually operates |
| Sales | The specific motion — B2C, high-ticket, multi-touch |

**Relevant education — 8 pts.** Preferably matched to the function:
- IT / automation / technical → engineering background
- Marketing → MBA, from a school with real teaching behind it
- Score on *relevance and what they actually learned*, not institutional prestige.
  A strong candidate from an unknown college beats a weak one from a known one.

**Clarity and ability to think — 7 pts.** Uday: *"the ability to think is very
important."* Can they reason through something they haven't seen before?
Test it directly — the practical exercise in each role's interview kit exists
for this.

---

## 3. COMMUNICATION — 30 points

Uday: *"communication is a very big important factor."* Weighted accordingly.

**Clarity of expression — 12 pts.** Can they get a thought across cleanly, in
speech and in writing? Not polish — clarity. Do you understand them the first
time?

**Understands priorities — 10 pts.** Grasps what matters to the organisation and
to their boss, and orders their own work accordingly. This is the difference
between someone who is busy and someone who is useful.

**Solution framing — 8 pts.** How they communicate a problem. Do they arrive with
the problem, or with the problem *and* a proposed path?

### How to assess

- The application text is a free writing sample. Use it.
- The phone screen is the real test for spoken clarity.
- For any customer-facing role, a voice note beats a page of CV.

## Bands

| Total | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | ⭐ Strong Hire |
| 68–79 | ✅ Hire |
| 55–67 | 🟡 Keep for Review |
| 40–54 | 🔵 Future Opportunity |
| Under 40 | ❌ Reject |

### The override that matters

**A candidate scoring below the interview bar on aptitude but visibly 9/10 on
attitude should be surfaced, not rejected.** Aptitude is teachable; attitude is
not. Flag these as *"low score, high attitude — worth a conversation"* and let
Uday decide. This is the single most likely place a good Befach hire gets lost.

The reverse does not apply. High aptitude with poor attitude is a reject.

---

## Bias guards — unchanged and non-negotiable

No scoring on name, gender, age, photo, marital status, or employer brand
recognition. Education is scored on **relevance to the function**, never on
institutional tier. Employment gaps are an interview question, never a
deduction — and in a "tried and failed" profile, a gap may be the most
interesting thing on the page.

---

## For future roles

When a new role opens, do not write a rubric from scratch. Take this file as the
frame, and build only the **role-specific 20-point aptitude block** — the tools,
platforms, domain knowledge and education that particular job needs. Attitude
and Communication stay exactly as written.
