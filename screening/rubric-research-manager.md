# Screening Rubric — Research Manager

100 points. Weighted toward **research you personally designed and ran** and
**decisions your research changed** — because the failure mode in this pool is
not incompetence, it is people who have spent four years formatting someone
else's findings.

## Gate 0 — Hard filters (fail = auto-Reject, no scoring)

| Filter | Rule |
|---|---|
| Location | Cannot work on-site in `[CITY]` and not willing to relocate |
| Ownership gate | Has never designed a study or scoped a research question themselves — only executed instructions, formatted decks, or ran tabs |
| Numeracy gate | Cannot demonstrate any quantitative work at all: no sizing, no survey analysis, no data handling |
| Compensation | Expected fixed CTC more than 25% above the top of our band |
| Completeness | No CV, or Q9 (the end-to-end research question) blank or pure filler |

> **Deliberately not a filter:** the research *category*. Someone who has done
> rigorous FMCG or telecom research can learn real estate in a quarter. Someone
> who has done shallow research in your exact category cannot learn rigour in a
> quarter. Category is scored (criterion 3), never gated.

## Scoring

### 1. Research craft and method depth — 25 pts
*Source: Q6, Q7, Q9. Can they choose a method and defend it?*

| Evidence | Pts |
|---|---|
| Q9 names the method **and the reason for it**, shows awareness of sample, bias and limitation, and covers both primary and secondary work | 21–25 |
| Solid method described, one side of the craft strong (primary or secondary), limitations acknowledged | 15–20 |
| Describes what they did without explaining why that method; method choice appears to have been handed to them | 9–14 |
| Executed fieldwork or desk research to instruction only | 4–8 |
| No discernible methodology | 0–3 |

> Listen for the word "because". A researcher who says "we used a survey because
> we needed to size an attitude we'd already heard in interviews" is a different
> population from one who says "we did a survey of 500 respondents".

### 2. Decisions changed — 20 pts
*Source: Q9's final part. The single most predictive item on this form.*

| Evidence | Pts |
|---|---|
| Names a specific decision — a price, a location, a segment, a launch killed — that moved because of their finding, with the direction and ideally the number | 17–20 |
| Findings clearly went to a decision maker and were acted on, described less concretely | 12–16 |
| Delivered to stakeholders; unclear whether anything happened | 6–11 |
| Output was a report or deck; no line to any decision | 2–5 |
| Cannot connect research to any outcome | 0–1 |

> This is where the strongest and weakest candidates separate most cleanly, and
> it is nearly impossible to fake in writing. Weight it accordingly.

### 3. Category relevance — 15 pts
*Source: Q8, CV. Relevant, not required.*

| Background | Pts |
|---|---|
| Real estate, housing, lending/BFSI, insurance, or high-ticket consumer finance research | 13–15 |
| Another high-ticket considered purchase with a human closing the sale — autos, education, healthcare, B2B capital goods | 10–12 |
| Rigorous consumer research in FMCG, retail, telecom, consumer durables | 7–9 |
| Research in a category with very different mechanics — SaaS, gaming, adtech | 4–6 |
| Academic or non-commercial research only | 1–3 |
| Unrelated | 0 |

### 4. Data and tooling fluency — 15 pts
*Source: Q7, Q10.*

| Evidence | Pts |
|---|---|
| Advanced spreadsheets **plus** SQL or a BI tool or Python/R, and has handled a messy real dataset end to end | 13–15 |
| Advanced spreadsheets, comfortable with survey platforms and statistical tooling (SPSS, R, or equivalent) | 9–12 |
| Competent spreadsheets, standard analysis | 5–8 |
| Basic spreadsheet use, mostly PowerPoint | 2–4 |
| No demonstrable data handling | 0–1 |

### 5. Written synthesis — 15 pts
*Judge Q9 itself as the writing sample. This role's output is writing.*

| Evidence | Pts |
|---|---|
| Leads with the conclusion, evidence is specific and quantified, tight and free of jargon, would survive being forwarded to a founder unedited | 13–15 |
| Clear and well-organised, some padding or buried lede | 9–12 |
| Understandable but generic, heavy on process language ("conducted", "facilitated", "leveraged") | 5–8 |
| Hard to follow, or buzzword-dense with no content | 0–4 |

> A research manager who cannot write cannot do this job, however good their
> analysis is. I score this harder here than for any other role in this repo.

### 6. Primary research execution in India — 10 pts
*Source: Q6, Q11. Can they actually get the data, not just design the study?*

| Evidence | Pts |
|---|---|
| Personally run fieldwork or interviews in an Indian market, in the local language, and managed the practical mess of it — recruitment, refusals, translation, data quality | 8–10 |
| Run interviews or surveys personally, mostly remote or metro/English | 5–7 |
| Designed research but always outsourced execution to an agency | 2–4 |
| Desk research only | 0–1 |

## Bands

| Score | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 70–100 | **Interview** | Phone screen, then the exercise |
| 55–69 | **Strong maybe** | Interview if the top band is thin — check criterion 2 first |
| 40–54 | **Maybe** | Hold |
| Under 40 | **Reject** | Courteous decline |

## The one thing that overrides the score

A candidate scoring in the 50s whose Q9 answer is *"the founder wanted to launch
in X, my fieldwork said the demand was in Y, we launched in Y and it worked"* —
interview them. Someone who has told a founder they were wrong, with evidence,
and been proven right, is exactly the hire. Tooling gaps are trainable; that
instinct is not. I will flag these as **"score says maybe, evidence says
interview"**.

## Red flag to surface, not auto-reject

Confident, precise-sounding market numbers with no stated source or method. In
this discipline that is the most common and most expensive failure mode, and it
looks identical to expertise on paper. I will flag it for the live sizing
exercise, which is designed to catch it.

## Bias guards

Same as the other two rubrics, with one addition specific to this pool: I do not
score on **employer brand**. Kantar and Nielsen on a CV are a positive signal
about training, not about ability — a strong in-house researcher at a company
you have never heard of routinely out-performs a mid-tier agency hire, because
they have had to make their own findings get used. Scored on evidence only: no
college tier, name, gender, age, photo, accent or institute.
