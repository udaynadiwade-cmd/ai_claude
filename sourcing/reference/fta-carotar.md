# FTA Preferential Claims & CAROTAR 2020 Defence

Claiming a preferential rate is not a tariff lookup — it is an evidentiary position you must be able
to defend at assessment and for years afterwards.

## Before you place the order

1. Confirm the product qualifies under the specific agreement's **product-specific rule** for that
   tariff line. The rule is usually one of:
   - **WO** — wholly obtained in the exporting country;
   - **CTH / CTSH** — change in tariff heading / sub-heading between inputs and output;
   - **RVC / VA%** — regional value content above a threshold;
   - a combination (e.g. "CTSH + 35% RVC").
2. Get the supplier to confirm **in writing** which criterion they will certify, and on what basis.
   A supplier who cannot answer this will not survive a CAROTAR query.
3. Price the deal **both ways** — at the preferential rate and at the MFN rate. If the origin
   evidence is thin, the MFN number is your real landed cost.

## Agreements in scope

AIFTA (ASEAN–India), India–UAE CEPA, India–Australia ECTA, India–Japan CEPA, India–Korea CEPA,
SAFTA, APTA, India–EFTA TEPA. Rules of origin differ materially between them — never carry an
assumption from one agreement into another.

## CAROTAR 2020 obligations on the importer

- **Form I information** must be in your possession *before* filing the Bill of Entry: origin
  criteria, production details, and the basis of the value-addition claim.
- Declare the preferential claim on the BoE and hold the **e-CoO** (verify issuance date, validity,
  signature/seal against the notified specimen, and any retrospective endorsement).
- **Transshipment cargo** (routed via Singapore, Colombo, Dubai etc.) needs a **non-manipulation
  certificate** from the transit port authority. Without it the claim fails regardless of the CoO.
- Maintain origin records for the prescribed retention period and produce them on a verification
  request within the prescribed timeline. Non-response is treated as withdrawal of the claim.

## What failure costs

Denial of preference, differential duty with interest, and penalty — assessed after the goods are
sold, when you can no longer reprice. Where origin evidence is weak, pay MFN and clear, or seek
provisional assessment; do not claim preference speculatively to hit a margin target.

## Contract clause

> The Seller shall furnish a valid preferential Certificate of Origin issued by the competent
> authority of the exporting country, together with the origin-criterion cost breakdown required
> under CAROTAR 2020, prior to shipment. Where a verification request under CAROTAR 2020 is
> received, the Seller shall furnish the supporting production and value-addition records within
> fifteen (15) days. Any differential duty, interest or penalty arising from an invalid,
> incomplete or unsubstantiated origin claim shall be to the Seller's account and may be set off
> against amounts payable.
