# Incoterms 2020, Ports & Container Cost Control

## Choosing the term

| Term | Risk/cost passes at | Use it when | Watch |
|------|--------------------|-------------|-------|
| **EXW** | Seller's premises | You have a strong forwarder in the origin country and want full route control | You inherit export clearance, which a Chinese supplier is usually better placed to do |
| **FOB** | On board at origin port | **Default for sea freight from China.** You control the carrier, the freight rate and the free-time negotiation | Confirm which origin local charges the supplier absorbs — "FOB" plus a THC surprise is common |
| **CFR / CIF** | On board (risk), seller pays freight (and insurance for CIF) | Small shipments where you have no forwarder relationship | You lose control of free days and destination charges; the supplier's "cheap" freight is often recovered at destination |
| **DAP** | Named destination, duty unpaid | Door delivery where you still want to file your own BoE and take IGST credit | Confirm who pays detention if clearance is delayed |
| **DDP** | Named destination, duty paid | Rarely appropriate for India | The importer of record must have an IEC and GSTIN. A DDP shipment cleared in someone else's name means **you cannot take IGST input credit** and you have no BoE for IDPMS. Treat DDP into India as a red flag |

## Free time — negotiate before booking, not after

- Ask for **14–21 free days combined detention and demurrage** at destination as a booking
  condition, written into the rate sheet.
- Demurrage (port) and detention (container) are separate clocks with separate tariffs. Get both in
  writing.
- Slot the customs timeline against free time: BoE filing, assessment/query cycle, examination,
  duty payment, out-of-charge, transport. A single faceless-assessment query can eat a week.

## Documentation

| Document | Failure mode |
|----------|--------------|
| **Original BL (OBL)** | Courier loss stalls delivery order. Prefer **telex release / surrendered BL** once payment terms allow |
| **Sea waybill** | Non-negotiable — only where payment risk is already covered |
| **Master vs. House AWB/BL** | Consolidator's HBL is only as good as the consolidator. Check the NVOCC's standing at destination |
| **Packing list** | Must reconcile to the invoice and the BoE line-wise, or examination stalls |
| **Certificate of Origin** | Preferential vs. non-preferential are different documents — see `fta-carotar.md` |

## Ports and ICDs

Gateway ports: Nhava Sheva/JNPT (INNSA1), Mundra (INMUN1), Chennai (INMAA1), Kolkata (INCCU1),
Pipavav (INPAV1), Cochin, Kattupalli. Inland: Tughlakabad (INTKD6), Patparganj, Bengaluru (Whitefield),
Hyderabad (Sanathnagar), Ludhiana.

Choosing an ICD moves clearance inland but adds a rail/road leg and a second free-time clock — model
the total, not just the ocean freight.

## Pre-arrival discipline

- File the **advance Bill of Entry** ahead of vessel arrival; late filing attracts charges under
  Section 46 of the Customs Act. Confirm the current timeline and charge with your CHA.
- Send the CHA the full document set at least 72 hours before ETA: invoice, packing list, BL,
  CoO, insurance, BIS/EPR/WPC references, and any licence.
- Track the vessel independently (MarineTraffic/VesselFinder) rather than relying on the
  forwarder's ETA.
