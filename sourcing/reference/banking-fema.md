# Payments, FEMA & IDPMS

## Standard payment structure

| Milestone | Share | Trigger |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Advance | 30% | Signed PI/contract, tooling release |
| Balance | 70% | PSI pass certificate + BL copy (telex release on receipt) |

Variants worth using:
- **Sight LC** where the supplier is new and the order is large — documentary control replaces trust.
- **Usance LC (e.g. 60/90 days)** to finance the working-capital gap between arrival and sale.
- Reduce the advance to 20% and add a 10% tranche at FAI approval when tooling is involved.

Never structure 100% advance, and never remit against an emailed change of bank details.

## Regulatory obligations at the Indian end

- Remit through your **AD Category-I bank**, quoting the correct purpose code. The bank reports the
  outward remittance into **IDPMS**.
- IDPMS matches the remittance against the **Bill of Entry** transmitted from ICEGATE. An unmatched
  entry ages into an outstanding, and persistent outstandings lead to **RBI caution-listing**, which
  blocks future remittances.
- So: after every clearance, send the CHA-supplied BoE to your bank and **confirm the IDPMS knock-off
  in writing**. Track it in a register alongside the remittance date.
- Advance remittances have prescribed timelines for submitting evidence of import — track each open
  advance and close it, or seek an extension before it ages.
- Keep the entity chain consistent: the beneficiary of the remittance, the shipper on the BL, and
  the seller on the invoice should be the same legal entity, or the difference should be documented
  (e.g. a named export agent) before you pay.

## Documents to file per shipment

Proforma invoice • purchase contract • commercial invoice • packing list • BL/AWB • insurance policy
• CoO • PSI report • Bill of Entry • remittance advice (SWIFT MT103) • IDPMS closure confirmation.
Keep them as one bundle per PO — this is what an audit, an SVB reference, or a CAROTAR verification
will ask for.
