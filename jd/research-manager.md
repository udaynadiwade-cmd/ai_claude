## About the role

We sell a high-ticket, considered purchase. Every decision we make about
pricing, location, positioning and which customer to chase is currently made on
instinct and on what the last customer said. That works until it doesn't.

This role exists to replace instinct with evidence — and to do it fast enough to
be useful. You will size markets, map competitors, understand why people buy
from us and why they walk, and turn all of it into decisions someone actually
takes on Monday.

This is not a role where you produce a quarterly deck that nobody reads. You
will sit between sales and marketing, and the test of your work is whether it
changed what those two teams did next week.

## What you will do

**Market and demand**

- Size the market we operate in and the ones we are considering — top-down and
  bottom-up, and reconcile the two when they disagree.
- Build and maintain a live competitor map: who they are, what they charge, what
  they promise, how they sell, and where they are weak.
- Track pricing in the market and tell us when to move, and by how much.
- Evaluate new locations, segments or product lines before we commit money to
  them: demand, competition, willingness to pay, and the honest downside case.

**Customer and buyer research**

- Run primary research directly — customer interviews, buyer surveys, lost-deal
  interviews, field visits. You will be on the phone and in the field yourself,
  not only briefing an agency.
- Own the lost-deal debrief: every deal we don't win gets a reason, and the
  reasons get counted.
- Build and keep current the buyer profile: who they are, what triggers the
  purchase, what they compare us against, what kills the deal.
- Turn qualitative signal into something countable. "Customers care about trust"
  is not a finding. "61% of lost deals cited a specific trust concern we can name
  and fix" is.

**Secondary research and data**

- Work industry data sources, government and regulatory data, portal and listing
  data, credit and lending data, annual reports and filings.
- Where we buy data or commission an agency, write the brief, choose the partner,
  hold them to quality, and check their numbers rather than forwarding them.
- Keep a single source of truth for market numbers so the company stops quoting
  three different figures in the same meeting.

**Turning research into decisions**

- Publish findings as a one-page recommendation with the decision, the evidence,
  the confidence level, and what would change your mind. Long decks are optional;
  the one-pager is not.
- Feed sales the objection and competitor intelligence they need on live calls.
- Feed marketing the message, segment and channel evidence behind campaigns.
- Say clearly when the data does not support a conclusion. Being the person who
  says "we don't know yet, and here's what it would cost to find out" is a core
  part of this job, not a failure of it.

## What we are looking for

**Must have**

- 4–7 years in market research, consumer insights, business research, strategy
  or consulting — with research you personally designed and ran, not only
  research you coordinated.
- Both halves of the craft: **primary** (survey design, interviewing, sampling,
  fieldwork) and **secondary** (desk research, data sourcing, triangulation).
- Genuine numeracy. You are comfortable in Excel or Sheets at pivot, lookup and
  basic-statistics level, and you know why a sample of 40 self-selected
  respondents does not support the claim someone wants to make from it.
- The ability to write. Findings that cannot be read in five minutes do not get
  used, and this role is judged on use.
- Market sizing you can defend — we will ask you to do one live.

**Strongly preferred**

- Research in **real estate, lending/BFSI, insurance, or another high-ticket
  considered purchase** where a salesperson closes the deal.
- Time at a research or advisory firm — Kantar, Nielsen, Ipsos, Redseer,
  1Lattice, Blackbox, Anarock, Knight Frank, JLL, CBRE, Liases Foras,
  PropEquity, CRE Matrix or similar — or the in-house insights team of a
  consumer brand.
- SQL, or a BI tool (Power BI, Looker Studio, Tableau), or Python/R for analysis.
  Not required, genuinely useful.
- Experience running fieldwork in `[CITY]` and the surrounding region, in the
  local language.
- Experience commissioning and managing external agencies and vendors.

**Not required**

- A specific degree or institute. Economics, statistics, MBA, urban planning,
  psychology and geography all produce good researchers. We care whether your
  conclusions held up.
