# The expertise gap — and the decision rule that resolves it

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** decision rule
**Prompted by:** no real leverage into dry fruit buyers; electricals needs category
expertise we do not have

---

## Dry fruits is out

You are right and it settles it. Rice dealers are not dry fruit buyers, and "both are
food" is a category-tree similarity, not a commercial one. The **only** argument for
dry fruits over electricals was a shorter time to first revenue through existing
relationships. That argument is now gone.

**Dry fruits drops off the list.** Do not revisit it.

## The expertise question, answered directly

You said electricals needs category expertise you do not have, but that it is
learnable. Both halves are true, and the second half matters more than you are
weighting it.

**Category expertise is the single most buyable input in this business.**

Consider who built the comparable companies:

| Company | Founders' background | Category expertise at founding |
|---|---|---|
| **Udaan** | Flipkart — e-commerce | None in FMCG wholesale |
| **Zetwerk** | ITC, consulting | Not manufacturers |
| **Infra.Market** | Finance, trading | Not building-materials people |
| **Moglix** | Google, Twitter | Not industrial MRO people |

Every one of them hired the category and supplied the operating system. **In Indian
B2B commerce, category expertise is a hire; distribution discipline, capital
allocation and go-to-market are the founder's job.** You have the second set. The
first is on the market.

### What that hire actually is

One person: 10–15 years in electrical distribution, ideally ex-Havells, Polycab,
Anchor, Legrand or a large regional distributor, based in Hyderabad. What they bring
that you cannot buy any other way:

- Which SKUs actually move, and which sit
- Live supplier and stockist relationships, and the credibility to open them
- The spec knowledge to tell compliant product from dangerous product
- Instinct for which counterparties pay

`ESTIMATE` — budget roughly ₹25–45 lakh per year for someone genuinely good at this
level in Hyderabad. Against a business you intend to take to several thousand crore,
that is not a cost, it is the entry fee.

### But in electricals the hire is a precondition, not a phase-two nice-to-have

This is where electricals differs from every other category on the list. **A defective
cable can burn a building down.** You cannot personally judge whether a consignment is
spec-compliant, and "I trusted my supplier" is not a defence when you are inside the
chain of supply.

In jewellery, being new costs you margin. In electricals, being new costs you safety
exposure. **Do not open this category without that person in the building.**

## The reframe that actually resolves your dilemma

Sort your gaps by whether they can be bought:

| Gap | Buyable? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Category expertise | **Yes** | One senior hire |
| Supplier relationships | **Yes** | Comes with the hire |
| Customer relationships | **Buildable** | Feet on the street, 90 days |
| Capital | **Raisable** | If the category story is big enough |
| Operating discipline | **You have it** | — |
| **Time** | **No** | This is the only one |

**Everything blocking electricals is purchasable except time.** Which means the correct
question is not *"which category do we already understand?"* — it is *"which category
can we hire into, fast enough?"*

---

## The decision rule

**Can you hire a strong electrical category head in Hyderabad within 60 days?**

**Yes → go electricals.** ₹8.7 lakh crore, documented availability pain, daily-purchase
urgency, no funded competitor. The expertise gap closes with one hire and the prize is
an order of magnitude larger than anything else available to you.

**No → go artificial jewellery, and size the ambition to match.** It is the one
category on the list where **you already have the expertise** — the required skill is
sourcing and design sense from China, which is precisely what you do. No BIS. No
FSSAI. No safety liability. No commodity position. Fastest time to first revenue of
anything discussed.

The honest trade-off: jewellery's ~₹42,000 Cr market supports maybe ₹2,000–4,000 Cr of
revenue at real share `ESTIMATE`. That is a genuinely good company. It is **not** a
Zetwerk-scale listing at ₹12,798 Cr or an Infra.Market at ₹18,472 Cr. If the IPO
ambition is firm at that scale, jewellery cannot get you there and electricals can.

## How to test the expertise gap for ₹2–3 lakh instead of a year

Do not hire first and test second. Do both at once:

**Engage an experienced electrical distribution person in Hyderabad as a consultant for
one month** — a retired or between-jobs sales manager from a large distributor or a
brand's channel team. `ESTIMATE` ₹2–3 lakh for the month.

Have them run the thirty-conversation field test **with** you. You get three things
for the price of one:

1. The field data on stockouts, credit and counterfeit
2. An honest read on whether the category is as open as it looks — they will know
   immediately why nobody has built this, if there is a reason
3. **A live audition of the exact person you would need to hire**, and a view of what
   that talent pool looks like in Hyderabad

If the consultant month goes well, you have your category head and your validation
together. If it goes badly, you have learned the category is harder than it looks, for
three lakh and four weeks.

**That is the next action.** Not more analysis — this one.
