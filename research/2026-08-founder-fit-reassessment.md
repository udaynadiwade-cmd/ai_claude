## Three things I had wrong

### 1. I was reading "sourcing" far too narrowly

I have been treating sourcing as *"we have China supplier relationships."* A **VP of a
Commodity Procurement Practice at Genpact** does something categorically different:
category management, **should-cost modelling**, spend analytics, supplier
rationalisation, negotiation strategy, and procurement transformation delivered to
large enterprise clients.

That is not a trading skill. It is a **consulting-grade discipline**, and it is rare in
Indian B2B distribution — where pricing is mostly relationship and haggling.

**Should-cost modelling in particular is a weapon.** Being able to tell a customer what
a part *ought* to cost, from first principles, is a proposition no local distributor can
answer and most cannot even parse.

### 2. I under-rated your fundability

When I set out the IPO clock I noted the path implies institutional capital and hedged
on whether that was available to you. On this profile I was too cautious.

**NIT Allahabad + XLRI + Genpact VP + Master Black Belt + PMP + two national awards,
with a co-founder carrying 30+ years of commodity trade** is a credible team for an
institutional round in Indian B2B commerce. Compare it to the founding teams that
raised in this sector — Udaan (Flipkart), Moglix (Google, Twitter), Infra.Market
(finance) — **none had procurement depth. They hired it.** You are it.

The constraint on raising is the thesis and the traction, not the team.

### 3. The MBB point is stronger than I made it

Master Black Belt **plus PMP plus enterprise procurement plus IT operations leadership**
is not "good at systems." It is the exact skill set for building a distribution
business that runs on measured service levels rather than instinct — and for
implementing it as a programme rather than an aspiration.

## The option this unlocks, which I under-weighted

### Managed procurement for mid-market Indian manufacturers

Not "we sell you goods." **"We run your indirect spend."**

- **Spend analysis** on their current indirect and MRO purchasing
- **Should-cost** benchmarking against what they actually pay
- **Supplier rationalisation** — 40 vendors down to 6
- **VMI** on the consumables that follow
- **Own-brand substitution** where it saves them money and makes you margin

| | |
|---|---|
| Uses | Sourcing ✓✓✓ Systems ✓✓✓ PMP ✓✓ Logistics ✓ E-comm ✓ Branding ✓ |
| Acquire-once | **Strongest of any model discussed.** Once you run a customer's spend you are inside their process, not on their vendor list |
| Entry wedge | **Spend analysis is a free, high-credibility first conversation.** It is also unfakeable — you either can do it or you cannot, and you can |
| Scale | Each customer worth ₹20 lakh–2 Cr of annual spend `ESTIMATE`; 500 customers is a serious company |
| Contested by | **Moglix** and **ProcMart** ($43.3M raised) `FACT` — both real, neither dominant, and neither founder-led by a procurement professional |
| Risk | Enterprise sales cycles are slow; you would be selling to procurement heads who are your professional peers — an advantage in credibility, a disadvantage in negotiation |

**This is the only model discussed where your expertise is the product rather than an
enabler.** Everything else asks you to be a distributor who happens to be good at
process. This asks you to be a procurement professional who distributes.
