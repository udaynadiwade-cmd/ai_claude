# Fresh global B2B ideas — blank slate scan

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** exploratory scan, fresh search
**Question:** what is genuinely new in B2B globally, and is any of it copyable to India?

---

## The honest headline finding

**Most of what is "new" in global B2B right now is software, not commerce.**

Y Combinator's Spring 2026 batch is **61% B2B (119 of 194 companies)**, but it leans
"heavily toward infrastructure and tooling rather than end-user apps" — including
"dozens of startups selling phone numbers, payments, identity, memory, sandboxes and
even insurance built specifically for AI agents." `FACT`

That is a different business from buying and selling goods. It does not use sourcing,
logistics, customs, or supplier relationships. **It is not Befach's game**, and saying
so is more useful than dressing up a software trend as a commerce opportunity.

---

## The four genuinely fresh models found

### 1. Deadstock / excess inventory marketplaces — the most interesting

- **Ghost** — members-only B2B marketplace for excess inventory, **raised $30M Series
  B** `FACT`
- **Forthclear** — B2B liquidation marketplace, free to list, **charges sellers 5%**
  on sale `FACT`
- **B-Stock** — one of the largest B2B liquidation networks; direct-sourcing model
  that removes intermediary liquidator markups `FACT`
- **BlueLots** — connects Amazon/Shopify/eBay resellers to bulk liquidation via live
  auctions

**Why the mechanism is interesting:** the seller is *motivated* — deadstock is dead
money sitting in a warehouse. That is a very different sales conversation from asking
someone to switch suppliers. No brand-loyalty fight. Usually cash, no credit.

### 2. AI-agent procurement

- **Lio** — **$30M Series A from a16z**, March 2026, end-to-end enterprise procurement
  automation via AI agents `FACT`
- **Levelpath** — $55M Series B, June 2025 `FACT`
- Context: **80% of B2B technology buyers already use AI agents** in purchasing (IDC,
  Aug 2026); Forrester expects ~1 in 5 B2B sellers to face agent-led quote
  negotiations by end-2026 `CLAIM — forecast, not measurement`

**Verdict for Befach: no.** This is enterprise SaaS. Different capability, different
customer, different sales motion. India also already has Procol here.

### 3. B2B equipment rental

- **233 startups** in the category, **50 funded**, 48 at Series A or beyond `FACT`
- KWIPPED, EquipmentShare, Tenderd, United Rentals, LOXAM, Sunbelt

**Verdict for Befach: no.** Either you own the equipment (asset-heavy, breaks the
asset-light rule outright) or you are a pure matcher carrying damage, insurance and
trust liability with no inventory control.

### 4. Freight and spot-procurement aggregation

- **Manifold** — aggregates spot freight opportunities from many shippers and
  brokerages into one place, cutting empty miles
- **FleetWorks** — rebuilding freight marketplace with AI

**Verdict for Befach: weak.** India already has Freight Tiger with Tata Motors money
behind it. Also not a goods business.

---

## The one worth a closer look — deadstock, applied to India

**Why India fits the mechanism unusually well:**
- Tiruppur generates **thousands of tonnes** of garment cutting waste `FACT`
- Surat's textile market has established **"balance stock"** deadstock merchants
  `FACT`
- Cancelled export orders are a chronic, structural source of good-quality surplus

**The competitor check, done properly:**
- **Refash** — e-commerce platform for deadstock garments, **130 brands** hosted, ~20%
  annual sales growth `FACT`. But this reads as **consumer-facing**, selling finished
  deadstock garments — not a B2B platform for factories offloading surplus fabric and
  stock to other businesses.
- The **B2B deadstock trade already exists and is active** — surplus garment and
  fabric wholesalers listed across IndiaMART and Justdial in Tiruppur and Surat.
- **So: no dominant B2B digital platform found, but a live offline trade.** Same
  pattern as Ranigunj bearings and Begum Bazaar electricals — the market is not empty,
  it is undigitised. `ESTIMATE — absence of a platform is not proof one is not being
  built; this needs a deeper check before acting on it.`

**How it scores against the standing criteria:**

| Criterion | Read |
|---|---|
| Asset-light | Yes — pure marketplace, seller holds the stock until sold |
| Cash, no credit | Yes — distress sales are typically cash |
| Repeat purchase | **Yes, and this is the non-obvious part** — the *factory* always has new deadstock, and the *discount buyer* always wants cheap stock. Both sides repeat even though each individual lot is one-off |
| Fragmented, unorganised | Yes — currently offline traders |
| Uses Befach's capability | **Partly, and this is the weakness** — it uses marketplace and logistics ability, but *not* the China sourcing and import machinery, which is the deepest existing asset |

**The honest problem with it:** it is a domestic-only business. It throws away the
import capability, the API access, and the two years of customs and shipping
experience — the things that actually distinguish Befach from a generic startup.

---

## What this scan does not do

It does not produce a better idea than the three-category import model already scored
in `2026-08-three-category-model-score.html`. Deadstock is a genuinely fresh
mechanism, but it is a different company from the one Befach has spent two years
building the ability to run.

**If deadstock is pursued, the honest framing is: this is a fresh start using
marketplace skill, not a continuation of the import business.** Worth saying plainly
rather than presenting it as an extension of what already exists.
