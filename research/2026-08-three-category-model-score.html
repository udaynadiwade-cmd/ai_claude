Model evaluation · catalog → order → own brand · August 2026

Catalog, order, brand

A free landed-price catalog brings people in. Some of them import through us. What they import tells us what to make under our own brand. Here is the score, and the two things that decide whether it works.

The model in three steps

Step 1 — Category C Free catalog Millions of products from 1688, each with a landed price in rupees. Earns nothing. Its only job is to bring buyers in.
Step 2 — Category B They import through us We handle sourcing, shipping, customs, delivery. We earn a margin on the shipment. This is the business we already run.
Step 3 — Category A Our own brand We now know what sells and how often. We make those products under the Befach name. Margin roughly triples.

Why the order matters: most companies guess what to private-label. This model makes the customers tell us first. That is the strongest idea here.

The scores

Ability to execute 7/10 We already do most of this. The catalog is the new part.
Profitability 6/10 Thin for two years. Good only if we reach the own-brand step.
Scalability 8/10 The catalog costs the same whether ten people use it or ten lakh.

Why each score

Ability to execute — 7 out of 10

  • In our favour: we already import. 100–150 shipments done. Customs, freight, payments all handled before.
  • In our favour: we have the 1688 API now. Last time we didn't. That was the blocker.
  • In our favour: about 100 existing clients. The catalog does not start empty of buyers.
  • Against us: a landed price needs the right HS code for every product. Millions of products means millions of code decisions. Get it wrong and the customer pays more than we quoted.
  • Against us: many Chinese products cannot legally enter India — BIS and QCO rules. Showing a price for a product nobody can import is worse than showing nothing.

Profitability — 6 out of 10

  • The catalog earns zero. It is a cost for as long as it runs.
  • Import service earns a real margin, and import margin is wider than local distribution margin.
  • Own brand is where the money is — roughly ₹16–18 per ₹100 against ₹6–7 for moving someone else's goods.
  • But own brand is two to three years away, and needs cash upfront. Chinese factories want 500 to several thousand units for custom branding.
  • So: thin money for a long time, good money only if we get all the way to step three.

Scalability — 8 out of 10

  • Once built, the catalog serves any number of people at almost no extra cost. This is the best part of the model.
  • People searching "landed price of X from China" find us on Google. Free customers, forever.
  • Limit: own-brand products do not scale the same way. Each one is a separate stock bet with its own money tied up.

The two things that decide whether this works

Risk one — the catalog can teach customers to skip us

If the catalog shows the supplier's name and price, a customer can contact that supplier directly. We would be paying to train our own competition. The bigger the customer, the more likely they do this — and the big customers are the ones worth keeping.

The fix is a design decision, and it must be made before building: show the product and the landed price, never the supplier identity. Our value is that we know who makes it. Give that away and nothing is left.

Risk two — we may never get enough data to pick a brand product

Own brand needs 500 to several thousand units of one product. That only makes sense if many customers buy the same item repeatedly. If 100 customers each import 100 different things, we learn nothing useful — we get variety, not concentration.

What to watch from day one: not how many orders we get, but how often the same product is ordered by different customers. That single number tells us whether step three is even possible.

Verdict

Worth doing — with one change

This is the best model discussed so far, for one reason: it is the only one that ends with us owning a product instead of only moving other people's goods. That is the escape from the ₹6–7 trap that killed Udaan and the others.

The change: do not treat step three as a distant hope. Set a date and a number now — for example, "by month 12, we must see 20 customers buying the same product, or we stop and rethink." Without that test, the catalog quietly becomes the whole business, and the catalog earns nothing.

Also note honestly: step three needs inventory. That breaks the asset-light rule set earlier. It is worth breaking for the margin — but it should be a decision made openly, not something we drift into.
