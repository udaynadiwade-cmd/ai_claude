# Decision memo — which business to actually build

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** recommendation, blunt version
**Prompted by:** "open to consider all business models afresh, no baggage"

## Three findings, in order of how much they should hurt

### 1. The CJ catalogue is not an asset

CJ Dropshipping is **free to anyone**. $0/month, open signup, public API, integrations
published for Shopify/WooCommerce/eBay/Amazon. `FACT`

There is no tie-up. There is an account. Every competitor can have the identical
catalogue by tomorrow morning, and several thousand already do.

Worse, the model underneath it does not work in India. **India has effectively no
de minimis** — the courier/postal threshold is ₹1,000, and BCD + IGST apply to
essentially every import regardless of value. `FACT` So each parcel carries duty,
IGST, courier clearance charges, 15–30 day transit and no practical returns path,
against Amazon and Meesho holding local stock at local prices.

Importing a generic catalogue also does active damage: thousands of SKUs with no
demand signal, no inventory, no differentiation, and a storefront that reads as a
dropshipping site rather than an import partner.

**Kill it.** Not deprioritise — delete the catalogue from befach.com.

### 2. The proposed model is the one you said you wanted to escape

You are pivoting off imports because of **scalability and risk**. "Help businesses
brand their own product categories through us" is private-label sourcing, which is
imports with:

- **more bespoke work per deal** — design, sampling, tooling, QC, compliance
- **more liability** — you are the agent on a product someone else puts their name on
- **longer cycles** — 2–6 months from enquiry to first container
- **worse disintermediation** — the client ends up knowing your factory *by name*,
  which is a deeper handover than a commodity order

And an MOQ problem that does not resolve: factories want 500–5,000 units for custom
packaging, and tooling on a custom-moulded product runs into thousands of dollars.
Indian SMEs asking for "our own brand" typically want 100–300 units. `ESTIMATE`
Someone absorbs that gap. It will be you, as inventory.

It is worse than what you have on both axes you named. That is the whole verdict.

### 3. You already own the best business in this conversation

**D'Cal.** You invented a product, got it manufactured, priced it at ₹3,600 against
₹30,000 conventional systems, put it on Amazon and Flipkart, and sell it across six
states. `FACT`

The India water softener market is roughly **$0.82–0.97 Bn and compounding at
8.6–9.8%**, and the published epicentre of Indian water hardness is South India —
**Hyderabad named explicitly**, because of borewell dependence. `FACT`

You own a differentiated product, in a large growing market, whose worst-affected
geography is the city you live in. And you are evaluating three different ways to
become a middleman.

## Recommendation

**Befach becomes a house of own brands for hard-problem Indian home and utility
products — sourced through your China capability, sold through marketplaces and your
own offline route, Hyderabad first.**

These are not three options. They compose into one company:

1. **Scale D'Cal.** Brand #1 already exists and already works. Everything else is
   secondary until this is either scaled or honestly written off.
2. **Add brands 2–4** in adjacent home/utility categories using the import capability
   as the supply engine. You have done this once; the second is cheaper.
3. **Build the offline route** from the category screen — incense, home care — which
   carries your own brands as well as regional ones. Hardware stores, plumbers and
   borewell servicers are a better channel for D'Cal than Amazon, and they cost you
   no marketplace take rate.

The import business is not the problem. **Selling other people's goods was the
problem.** Keep befach.com as a cash-generating sourcing arm and stop trying to make
it a marketplace.

## The one thing that could invalidate all of this

**Does D'Cal actually work, with third-party data?**

The published description — zero electricity, drops into a borewell, "removes the
stickiness of calcium ions" rather than removing minerals — is the profile of a
catalytic or physical water conditioner. That class of product has **contested
scientific support** relative to ion-exchange softening.

If there is NABL-accredited before/after test data, this is a defensible technology
story and the recommendation stands at full strength. If there is not, then scaling
it means scaling efficacy claims you cannot substantiate, which is a consumer-
protection and ASCI exposure that grows with every rupee of marketing spend — and the
recommendation inverts completely.

**I need to see the test data before anything else in this memo gets executed.**
