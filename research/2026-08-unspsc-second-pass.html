The cash trades

Your criteria this time are stricter and more specific than anything used before — no credit, fat margins, no price war, easy execution. That combination rules out most of what this series has recommended so far, and rewards a different kind of category.

What your criteria actually select for

Read together, these seven conditions describe one specific shape: a fragmented, high-value trade where the buyer is cash-flush by the norms of their own industry, currently served by many small unorganised suppliers, with no dominant B2B platform having consolidated it.

That is a narrower and different target than electricals or bearings. Both of those score well on size and fragmentation but score badly on "no credit" — the electrical trade runs on 15–30 day terms as an entry ticket, and industrial buyers expect it as standard. Your new constraint rules out most of what has been recommended so far in this series, and that is worth saying plainly before the new candidates.

The execution caveat, stated precisely rather than avoided

Surat's diamond trade runs on closed, familial community networks — historically dominated by specific Gujarati Jain and Patel family lineages, with trust built over generations and outsiders genuinely finding it hard to enter the trading side of the business.

But that closure applies to trading diamonds, not to selling grinding wheels and polishing compounds to the people who cut them. The suppliers found in this search — Karma Steels (Mumbai, established 1988), Sachi (Jaipur), Soham (Surat) — are ordinary industrial-goods companies, not community insiders, and they appear to sell into this trade successfully. You would be a tooling and consumables vendor, not a diamond trader. That is a materially easier door to walk through, though still one that rewards a credible industrial-supplier presentation over a cold digital pitch — this is a trade that transacts on trust, and a first sale likely needs a real person and a real sample, not a website.

Two others surfaced, and why they score lower on your specific brief

Textile machinery accessories / power loom spares

Real and large — fragmented across Bhiwandi, Surat, Malegaon, Ichalkaranji, Panipat, Coimbatore, Salem Fact. But power loom units are a well-documented financially stressed segment of Indian textiles — precisely the kind of buyer who needs credit, not the kind who pays cash. This fails your explicit no-credit condition even though it passes every other one, and that single failure is disqualifying against this brief specifically.

Leatherworking machinery and consumables

Ties to Kanpur, Agra and Chennai's leather export clusters. Export houses paid via letter of credit are genuinely cash-rich on the international side — but the domestic tooling and consumables purchase, made by the same units, does not automatically inherit that cash position; working capital gets absorbed by raw hide and finishing costs first. Worth a look, but unverified — the field test would need to ask this directly rather than assume it.

Recommendation

  1. Add gem-processing consumables to the field test — Surat if reachable, or Jaipur's coloured-gemstone cutting trade as a more accessible entry point with a similar profile and a shorter distance from Hyderabad.
  2. Ask the margin question directly — "what do you currently pay for polishing compound / laps / diamond powder, and from how many different suppliers" is the single question that would confirm or kill this in one conversation.
  3. Present as an industrial consumables supplier, not as anyone trying to enter the diamond trade itself. That framing is both more honest and the one that actually gets a meeting.
  4. Drop power loom spares from further consideration on the credit condition alone, despite the genuine scale and fragmentation.
  5. This does not replace bearings. It sits alongside it as a genuinely different kind of candidate — smaller, higher-margin, credit-free, execution-cautious — worth testing in parallel rather than instead.
