Business model · Electrical B2B, Hyderabad · August 2026

Stock nothing

Grainger's service model, run at ZKH's margins, delivered at Blinkit's speed, locked in with Fastenal's hardware — and made profitable by the one thing none of them started with.

The model, in one paragraph

An electrical goods supply company for Hyderabad's electricians, contractors and small retailers, whose product is not goods but the removal of their inventory. Dark stores hold the range no customer can afford to hold; a forty-five minute delivery promise makes holding stock unnecessary; managed cabinets on the biggest customers' premises make leaving impossible; and an own-brand line on the highest-repeat items turns a 14% gross margin into 20%.

The customer proposition is one sentence: "Stop carrying stock. Tell us what you need and it is with you in forty-five minutes."

The two tiers

Tier 1 · Speed

Dark store, 45 minutes

  • Customer: electricians, small contractors, electrical retailers
  • Range: 300–500 urgent SKUs, two-wheeler deliverable
  • Order: WhatsApp first, app later
  • Frequency: 2–3× per week
  • Job: acquisition and frequency

Switches, sockets, MCBs, holders, connectors, lugs, tape, cable ties, tools, short wire runs. No cable drums, no panels — those go on a next-day route.

Tier 2 · Depth

Managed cabinets on site

  • Customer: large contractors, MEP firms, factories, facilities
  • Range: their consumption profile, modelled
  • Order: none — you replenish on consumption
  • Frequency: weekly restock visit
  • Job: retention and margin

A stocked cabinet at their site, owned by you, billed on what they use. They stop ordering. You stop selling. Revenue becomes consumption.

And underneath both — the own brand

On the highest-repeat, lowest-brand-pull items: switch plates, boxes, PVC conduit, junction boxes, lugs, tape, cable ties, hand tools, basic fittings. Nobody asks for a brand of junction box.

Contract-manufactured domestically to start — which keeps BIS compliance with the manufacturer and avoids the QCO regime entirely — with China sourcing as the cost lever once volume justifies it.

The honest company-level number

Store EBITDA of 11.2% is before corporate overhead — tech, category management, private-label development, marketing, finance. Assume 5–6% of revenue at scale. Assumption

Company-level EBITDA lands around 5–6%. That is not Grainger's 13.9%. But set it against what Indian B2B actually delivers: OfBusiness 2.7% net, Infra.Market 1.2%, Udaan −23.1%. Fact Roughly double the best of them, and the difference is entirely private label.

For scale reference: Blinkit runs 2,243 dark stores. This needs 200.

Is ₹3,285 Cr enough for the listing you want? Zetwerk filed its DRHP at ₹12,798 Cr with a loss; Infra.Market at ₹18,472 Cr at 1.2% net. Fact You would be listing smaller but far more profitably — and profitable growth lists better than unprofitable scale. Adding the credit layer in year four, against transaction history you already own, is what closes the gap.

Read this before anything else in the document

Receivables are two-thirds of the capital requirement. Credit is not a feature of this business, it is the balance sheet. The electrical trade runs on 15–30 day terms and you cannot opt out.

Which means: this is a venture-funded path, not a bootstrapped one — and the fundraise is not optional, it is structural. Phase 1 costs lakhs. Phase 4 costs a few hundred crore.

Phasing

0Mo 0–3

Validate and hire

Thirty conversations in the Hyderabad electrical trade. Engage an experienced electrical distribution manager for a month to run them with you — field data, an insider read on why the category is unclaimed, and a live audition of your category head, for ₹2–3 lakh.

1Mo 3–9

One store, one zone, no app

1,500 sq ft. 300–500 SKUs. WhatsApp orders. Two bikes. Go condition: 50+ orders/day sustained four weeks at AOV above ₹2,000. Measure orders/day, AOV, delivery cost, 30-day repeat. Nothing else.

2Mo 9–18

Clone, brand, and place the first cabinets

4–6 Hyderabad zones. Launch own brand on the top 20 SKUs by repeat. Put managed cabinets with your ten best contractor accounts. Build the ordering app now — not before. Raise a seed or pre-A on measured cohort data.

3Mo 18–36

Own Hyderabad, then prove city two

12 stores across Hyderabad. Own-brand mix to 20%. Second city — the real test, because it is where you learn what actually transfers. Series A against two-city cohort data.

4Yr 3–7

Scale, and add credit

20–25 cities, ~200 stores, own-brand mix to 35%. Layer trade credit against your own transaction history — the Oxyzo move, which produces an estimated 57% of OfBusiness's profit from under 5% of its revenue. Fact Then file.

Why it is defensible

  • Own brand — they cannot buy it anywhere else, which is what breaks the disintermediation cycle that has ended every previous version of your business
  • Cabinets on their floor — removing you is a project, not a decision
  • Substitution intelligence — knowing what replaces the out-of-stock item, improving with every transaction
  • Fill rate as a measured service level — the trade runs on instinct; you would run on statistics, and that gap is your Six Sigma training doing the actual work
  • Credit — switching costs money once they owe you

What kills it

  • Blinkit or Zepto entering B2B. They have 2,000+ stores, riders and software. They lack electrical assortment, trade credit and supplier relationships. Assume two to three years of clear air, not ten.
  • AOV below ₹2,000. Contribution collapses and break-even triples. This is the single number to establish first.
  • The credit book going bad. Two-thirds of your capital is receivables. A bad vintage costs years, not quarters.
  • Drifting into cable. Wire is a copper position. Every rupee of cable inventory is a commodity bet you are not being paid to take. Fittings and switchgear only, at least at first.
  • Assortment creep. Every "can you also get me…" that you say yes to degrades the model toward being an ordinary distributor with an expensive promise attached. Udaan died of this.
  • A seventh pivot. The clock is 7–9 years and you are 57. This one has to be it.

The next thing to do

Not raise. Not build. Not hire a team.

One action

Thirty conversations in Hyderabad's electrical trade, with an experienced electrical distribution person alongside you, in the next four weeks.

Five questions: what did you need and not get today · what does an hour of waiting cost you · how do you know a cable is genuine · what credit do you get and from whom · who supplies you now and what is wrong with them.

Everything in this document is an assumption until those thirty conversations happen. They cost ₹2–3 lakh and four weeks. The model is worth exactly nothing until they are done.
