# Food B2B — evaluated honestly

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** category evaluation
**Question:** can we expand into food B2B — a cash business with a large opportunity?

---

## First, separate two things you are conflating

**Befach low-GI rice is a branded food product.** You already concluded that path is not
your forte, and you were right — it needs R&D, manufacturing relationships, and brand
marketing against entrenched staples players.

**Food B2B distribution is a completely different business.** You would be moving other
people's food to businesses, at a distribution margin, on a route.

Having the rice does not give you an entry into food B2B. What it gives you is **an
FSSAI licence, a supplier network and some category familiarity** — a head start on
paperwork, not a strategic position. Do not let the rice make food look easier than it
is.

---

## The map — two of the three big food spaces are already closed

| Space | Who owns it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Restaurant / HoReCa supply** | **HyperPure** — FY25 revenue **₹6,196 Cr, +95% YoY**, Q4FY26 adjusted EBITDA positive at ₹5 Cr `FACT` | **Closed.** Backed by Eternal/Zomato, which can fund losses indefinitely and already has the restaurant relationship through the delivery platform |
| **Kirana grocery** | **Jumbotail** — unicorn, $263M raised, **500,000+ kiranas and MSMEs across 400+ cities** after the Solv acquisition `FACT`. Plus Udaan, ElasticRun | **Closed.** And Udaan lost ₹1,055 Cr on ₹4,561 Cr proving how hard it is |
| **Fresh produce** | Ninjacart, WayCool | Avoid — wastage economics are punishing and WayCool's troubles are instructive |

You cannot enter either of the first two from a standing start in Hyderabad in 2026.
HyperPure grew 95% in a year and has a captive customer list from the parent's food
delivery business. That is not a fight worth having.

## What is actually open in food

### 1. Dry fruits and nuts — the best food fit for you specifically

| | |
|---|---|
| Market | **$9.3 Bn (2024) → $12.7 Bn (2029)**, 6.55% CAGR `FACT` |
| Import intensity | **India is the world's largest importer of dry fruits — $2.85 Bn in 2023.** US supplies 35%. Cashews alone are ~50% of import value `FACT` |
| Buyers | Dry fruit shops, sweet shops, kirana, gifting companies, caterers, bakeries |
| Cash cycle | Better than restaurants — retail and sweet-shop trade is largely cash or short credit |
| Shelf life | Long. **No cold chain** |
| Value density | High — cheap to move, cheap to hold per rupee of value |
| Seasonality | Festive and wedding demand spikes — real, and plannable |

**This is the only food category where your import capability is a genuine edge rather
than a nice-to-have.** In every other food space you would be a distributor of Indian
goods competing with people who have done it for thirty years. Here, the country's
supply is structurally import-dependent, and sourcing is the job.

**The catch, and it is the same one as electricals:** *price volatility from commodity
market fluctuations* is a named top challenge in the sector `FACT`. You would be long
almonds and cashews the way an electrical distributor is long copper. Inventory value
moves against you.

### 2. Bakery ingredients — the best buy-to-operate structure in food

| | |
|---|---|
| Market | India bakery **$15.05 Bn (2025) → $32.05 Bn (2034)**, 8.76% CAGR `FACT` |
| Fragmentation | **Over a million unorganised small-scale bakeries**; ~100,000 semi-organised; only ~2,000 organised. **Over 70% of the market is unorganised** `FACT` |
| Buying behaviour | **Buy to operate.** Flour, sugar, yeast, fats, cocoa, essences, colours, packaging — bought weekly or more often, non-discretionary, zero trend risk |
| Who serves them today | Local ingredient wholesalers and cash-and-carry. Thinly served, no digital player of scale |

A million unorganised bakeries is the most fragmented buyer base I have found in any
category across this whole survey, and they buy consumables on a fixed clock. That is
textbook painkiller structure.

**The catch:** fats and dairy need cold chain, commodity ingredients (flour, sugar,
oil) are thin-margin, and FSSAI liability attaches to every line.

### 3. Your rice, as a product line — not a business

India's diabetic population makes low-GI a real and growing niche. But it is a
**product to carry on a route you build for other reasons**, not a business that
reaches IPO scale on its own. Treat it as an asset, not a strategy.

---

## The compliance load you would be re-accepting

You pivoted partly to escape compliance. Food re-imports it:

- **Distributors and wholesalers need their own FSSAI licence**, and operating without
  carries up to **six months' imprisonment and ₹5 lakh**; selling food "not of the
  nature, substance or quality demanded" carries a further penalty up to ₹5 lakh `FACT`
- **Expiry-dated stock** means write-offs are a permanent line item, and regional
  suppliers rarely accept returns from a new distributor
- **Recall exposure** on anything you have touched

This is manageable — thousands of licensed distributors operate fine — but it is a
compliance function you must build and staff, and it is the opposite direction from
where you said you wanted to go.

**Electricals is still roughly eleven times the market of dry fruits**, with higher
purchase urgency and no expiry. Dry fruits wins on one axis only — but it is the axis
you keep coming back to, which is that your sourcing capability finally becomes a real
edge instead of an internal tool.

---

## Recommendation

**If you go into food, go into dry fruits and nuts. Do not go into restaurant supply or
kirana grocery at any price.**

But my honest ranking is unchanged: **electricals first, dry fruits second, and only if
you have a specific reason to prefer food** — such as an existing supplier network from
the rice business, or a Hyderabad dry-fruit trade relationship you have not mentioned.

The single argument that would flip me to dry fruits: **if you already have working
import lanes, an FSSAI licence, and buyer relationships in the food trade, the time to
first revenue is materially shorter than electricals.** Speed matters at your timeline.
Tell me if that is the case and I will re-rank.

---

## The thing I would be failing you if I did not say

This is the **sixth direction** in this conversation: import platform → regional brand
distribution → own brands → auto aftermarket → electricals → food.

Each exploration has been genuinely useful and the thinking has sharpened every time.
But the clock from the earlier memo has not moved: **7–9 years from start to DRHP in
this sector, and you are 57.** The binding constraint is no longer which category is
best — the differences between the top three are smaller than the cost of another six
months deciding.

**The field test costs three weeks and settles this empirically.** Thirty conversations
in Hyderabad's electrical market and thirty in the dry fruit trade at Begum Bazaar.
That is a fortnight of two people's time and it replaces every judgement in this
document with evidence.

**I would rather you spend three weeks testing than three more months choosing.**
