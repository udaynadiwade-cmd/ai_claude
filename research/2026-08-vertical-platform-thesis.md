# Vertical B2B platform — the thesis, and the clock

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** recommendation
**Prompted by:** pivot from product to platform; goal is big revenue and IPO; founder is 57

---

## What you got right

**1. Vertical over horizontal.** Correct, and it is the single most important call in
this document. Udaan went horizontal and lost ₹1,055 Cr on ₹4,561 Cr revenue in FY25.
Every profitable operator in the set is locked to one buyer type. Your reasoning —
end-to-end supply to one vertical so CAC amortises across wallet share — is exactly
right and is the reason the imports business was not working. You were solving 5% of a
customer's problem and competing for attention with everyone solving the other 95%.

**2. Platform over product.** Also correct, for you specifically. Product companies
need R&D and manufacturing. You have neither and you have said so. Kent is a fair
ceiling estimate for that path and it takes 20 years to reach.

**3. Revenue scale is the IPO ticket in this sector.** This one surprises people, but
you are right. **Zetwerk filed its DRHP with ₹12,798 Cr revenue and a ₹371 Cr net
loss.** Infra.Market filed to raise ₹5,000 Cr at ₹18,472 Cr revenue and 1.2% net
margin. `FACT` Indian public markets will absorb a low-margin B2B distributor at
scale. Your instinct about the revenue league is not naive — it is how this sector
actually lists.

## What you got wrong, and it matters

> *"Adding more products makes us a product company that has to get into
> manufacturing and R&D."*

**No. You are conflating two completely different activities.**

- **Inventing D'Cal** — product company. R&D, testing, manufacturing, IP. Not your
  forte, correctly abandoned.
- **Private-labelling a commodity SKU inside a distribution platform** — a *sourcing*
  activity. No R&D. No lab. No factory. You find a contract manufacturer, put your
  name on an existing product, and sell it through a channel you already own.

Infra.Market does **₹18,472 Cr** of revenue on private-label building materials and
runs no R&D function. It brands tiles, plywood, cement and sanitaryware — commodities
someone else already knows how to make.

**That is literally your forte.** Sourcing plus logistics plus sales. You already do
it, in China, today.

This matters because private label is not a distraction from the platform thesis — it
is **the only thing that makes a distribution platform profitable enough to list.**
Without it you are Udaan at −23%. With it you are Infra.Market. Same sector, 25 points
of margin apart.

## The clock — the part nobody will say to you

| Company | Founded | DRHP filed | Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zetwerk | 2018 | 2025 | 7 |
| Infra.Market | 2016 | 2025 | 9 |
| Udaan | 2016 | not yet | 10+ |

**Seven to nine years is the realistic B2B commerce IPO clock.** Starting now at 57
puts listing at 64–66. That is achievable.

It is achievable **only if this is the last pivot.** Each pivot costs 12–18 months and
resets the clock — and you have made three in this conversation alone. The thing most
likely to prevent the IPO is not the vertical you choose. It is choosing a fourth
vertical in 2027.

**You have one pivot left. Spend it deliberately.**

Second hard truth: you cannot bootstrap to ₹5,000 Cr of distribution revenue. Working
capital scales with GMV. Infra.Market and Zetwerk both raised heavily. **The IPO goal
implies an institutional fundraising path**, which means the vertical has to be big
enough to carry a venture story, and you need proof of repeat and take rate within
18 months to raise the round after seed.

## The vertical: automotive aftermarket

| Criterion | Auto aftermarket | Pet care |
|---|---|---|
| TAM | **₹99,948 Cr** (FY25) `FACT` | ~$1–2.5 Bn pet food; ~$8.6 Bn pet care products — *estimates vary wildly across sources, treat with suspicion* |
| Fragmentation | Extreme — unorganised retailers, informal distributors, roadside garages `FACT` | High |
| Repeat | Very high — consumables and wear parts | Very high — food monthly |
| China import edge | Very strong | Strong |
| Private-label headroom | Very strong | Very strong |
| Compliance load | Low–moderate for non-safety parts | Moderate — pet food import needs quarantine clearance |
| R&D required | None | None |
| Execution difficulty | **High** — fitment complexity | Moderate |

**Auto wins on the only axis that matters for your stated goal.** At ₹1 lakh crore,
5% share is ₹5,000 Cr and 10% is ₹10,000 Cr — Infra.Market and Zetwerk territory, and
a credible listing story. Pet care's ceiling is roughly a third of that; it is the
better business if you want to be comfortably profitable in 24 months, and the wrong
one if you want to list.

**And the counterfeit problem is your opening.** Counterfeit parts are named as a
defining problem of this market. `FACT` A garage owner cannot verify what he is
buying. Verified, traceable, warrantied supply is a real value proposition — not a
convenience play — and it is exactly what an importer with QC discipline can offer.

### The wedge — avoid the hard problem for 18 months

The genuine difficulty in auto parts is **fitment**: the make / model / year / variant
matrix. It is years of data work and it is where most entrants die.

**So do not start there.** Start with the large fitment-agnostic subset:

lubricants · car care chemicals · batteries · wipers · bulbs and lighting ·
filters (partial) · accessories · workshop consumables · tools · cleaning and
detailing

These need no fitment matrix, are high-repeat, are heavily imported, and are
straightforwardly private-labelable. You build the garage relationships, the route,
the ordering platform and the private-label line on easy SKUs — then add the fitment
catalogue in year two when you have the customer base to justify it.

## The model, precisely

**A vertical B2B commerce platform for the automotive aftermarket: end-to-end supply
to garages, workshops and parts retailers, with a private-label layer sourced through
your existing China capability.**

Four layers, each mapping to a forte you actually have:

| Layer | What it does | Your forte |
|---|---|---|
| Catalogue + ordering | The platform garages order from | Tech |
| Supply | Multi-brand distribution, verified, no counterfeits | Sourcing |
| Fulfilment | Same-day / next-day to the workshop | Logistics |
| **Own brand** | Private-label consumables — **the margin engine** | Sourcing |

Your branding idea was right and pointed at the wrong beneficiary. **Do not help other
businesses build their brands. Build yours, inside your own vertical, as the margin
layer.** That is the difference between a 2% business and a 12% one.

Later, once you have transaction history: credit. Oxyzo produces an estimated 57% of
OfBusiness's profit from under 5% of its revenue. Garages are chronically
underfinanced. But that is year three — you need the book and the data first.

## What kills this

1. **A fourth pivot.** The single biggest risk. Everything else is manageable.
2. **Fitment scope creep.** Adding the full parts matrix in year one will consume the
   company. Hold the line on consumables.
3. **Counterfeit liability.** You will be offered cheap parts through grey channels.
   Buying once, from inside the liability chain, converts a business into a legal
   problem. Direct from manufacturer, on invoice, always.
4. **Credit.** Garages will ask for terms. Unlike the kirana model, in auto this is
   near-unavoidable — budget for it and price it, do not pretend it away.
5. **Working capital underestimation.** Parts distribution is inventory-heavy. Model
   this before committing, not after.
6. **Raising.** If institutional capital is not available, the honest answer is that
   pet care at a smaller scale is the better business, because it can be built from
   cash flow.
