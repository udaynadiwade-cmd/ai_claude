# India's import problems — who hurts most, and which category

**Date:** August 2026 · **Status:** fresh research
**Question:** what are the real problems in Indian importing, who suffers most, which
category should we pick?

---

## The problems, ranked by what the evidence actually says

### 1. HS code classification — the number one cause of delays

**"The most frequent cause of clearance delays is HS Code mismatch — where the
product's actual classification doesn't match the declared HS code."** `FACT`

This is the most important finding in this note. It is **a knowledge problem, not a
money problem.** Nobody needs capital to fix it. Someone just has to know the right
code.

### 2. BIS and QCO certification — the biggest wall, by far

| | |
|---|---|
| Cost to certify **one product** | **₹15–20 lakh** `FACT` |
| Time taken | **6–8 months** `FACT` |
| What importers pay for | Performance bank guarantees, testing fees, licensing fees, annual renewals, **and travel costs for BIS officials to inspect the overseas factory** `FACT` |

MSMEs have publicly called these orders costly and restrictive. `FACT`

**But note the scale:** of **over 23,000 BIS standards, only 187 have QCOs** — mostly
**steel and electronics.** `FACT` So most products are not caught by this. Newly added:
**industrial chemicals** (staggered through 2026) and **furniture** — the furniture
order "caught many domestic manufacturers off guard." `FACT`

### 3. Working capital and delayed payments

MSMEs face hard credit access, delayed payments, rising compliance costs. Many depend
on imported inputs, exposing them to shipping delays, currency swings and geopolitical
shocks. `FACT`

### 4. Documentation complexity

Complex customs paperwork is "overwhelming for small enterprises with limited
resources." `FACT`

### 5. Port congestion and shipment uncertainty

2026 specifically: changing compliance policy, port congestion, seasonal delays,
freight limitations. `FACT`

---

## Two 2026 government changes that matter

1. **Transition Facilitation (Quality Control) Order, 2026** — an alternative
   compliance route letting eligible manufacturers get BIS licences for **imported
   components** without the traditional overseas factory inspection. `FACT` This
   directly attacks the ₹15–20 lakh barrier.
2. **CBIC deferred customs duty EMI scheme, 2026** — lets duty be paid in instalments.
   `FACT` Directly attacks the working capital problem.

Both are recent. Both reduce the exact barriers that made importing hard. `ESTIMATE`
Nobody has built a service around explaining and using these yet — worth checking
properly.

---

## Who faces the maximum problems

| Who | Level of pain | Can they pay? | Do they repeat? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Large manufacturers** | Low — they have import departments and in-house customs teams | Yes | Yes |
| **Large retailers** | Low — same | Yes | Yes |
| **MSME manufacturers** | **High** — import inputs regularly but have no customs expertise, cannot afford ₹15–20 lakh certification | Slowly | **Yes** |
| **Small traders / retailers** | **Highest** — import finished consumer goods, which are the most regulated | Cash, but small | Weak — often one-off |
| **First-time importers** | Highest of all | Small amounts | No |

**Maximum pain sits with small traders and first-time importers. Maximum
*opportunity* does not.**

---

## The uncomfortable insight

**The categories with the most pain are the ones we also cannot serve.**

If a product needs BIS certification costing ₹15–20 lakh and 6–8 months, **that cost
falls on us too** the moment we become the importer. We do not escape it by being a
platform. Steel, electronics, furniture and now industrial chemicals are the worst-hit
categories — and they are worst-hit for us as well.

**So do not chase the loudest pain.** Chase the pain we can actually remove.

---

## What that leaves — the recommendation

**Industry: MSME manufacturers. Category: repeat-purchase industrial inputs that are
NOT on the QCO list.**

**Why MSME manufacturers, not traders:**
- They buy the same thing again and again — traders buy one-off
- They import to produce, not to resell, so no brand-loyalty fight
- They lack customs knowledge, which is exactly what we have
- ~**129,901 active importers** exist in India, so the buyer pool is real `FACT`

**Why non-QCO products specifically:**
- 187 of 23,000 standards carry QCOs — **most products are clear**
- Avoiding those 187 removes the single biggest cost and delay in the whole process
- It turns a ₹15–20 lakh, 6–8 month problem into a normal shipment

**The first thing to sell them — and it is not goods:**

**HS code classification and landed-cost accuracy.** It is the number one cause of
delays, it needs no capital, no licence, no inventory, and it is exactly what our two
years of shipments taught us. It also connects directly to the "smallest service"
recommendation already made — sourcing and quoting, goods never touched.

---

## What to verify before acting

1. **Get the actual QCO list of 187 products.** Everything above depends on knowing
   precisely which products are caught. This is a published government list — get it,
   do not estimate it.
2. **Read the Transition Facilitation QCO 2026 order properly.** If it genuinely
   removes the factory-inspection requirement for imported components, it changes which
   categories are viable.
3. **Check the CBIC EMI duty scheme eligibility.** If our customers qualify, their
   working capital objection weakens, which makes them easier to sell to.
