## The seven tests a trade brand name has to pass

Not generic branding advice — these are the tests that matter for a company selling
cross-border trade services to businesses.

1. **Directional neutrality** — does it work for goods coming IN *and* going OUT?
2. **Buyer signal** — does it sound like it sells to businesses, or to consumers?
3. **Phone test** — can a salesperson say it once and have it spelled correctly?
4. **Cross-border test** — can a Chinese supplier and a foreign buyer say it?
5. **Legal test** — is it registrable, and can you stop a copycat?
6. **Domain test** — do you own the version a global buyer will type first?
7. **Story test** — does the name carry the strategy, or is it just a label?

|---|---|---|---|

## Global Shopper — drop it. Four independent reasons.

### 1. It contradicts half the business

The trade flows **both ways**. **An exporter is not a shopper.** When Befach sells
Indian goods out to a buyer abroad, the name describes the customer's activity, not the
company's. **A name that denies half of what you do is not a small problem.**

### 2. "Shopper" signals a consumer, and you sell to manufacturers

Your buyer is a factory with a GST number placing repeat industrial orders. "Shopper"
signals a person browsing for a bargain.

This is not cosmetic. **A name sets the price expectation before the first
conversation.** "Shopper" primes discount-hunting. You are trying to sell a *guarantee*
— fixed landed price, customs handled — which is a premium promise. **The name argues
against your own pricing.**

### 3. It is probably not registrable — this is the decisive point

**Section 9 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999** refuses marks that are **devoid of
distinctive character**, or that consist exclusively of indications describing the
**kind, quality, quantity, intended purpose or geographical origin** of the goods or
services. `FACT`

**"Global Shopper" for a global shopping service is exactly that** — descriptive of
purpose and origin, in plain English.

There is an escape route: descriptive marks *can* be registered on proof of **acquired
distinctiveness**, evidenced by sales turnover, advertising spend, market surveys and
industry recognition. `FACT` **You do not have that at ₹0.5 crore of import revenue.**
It is a defence available to Nestlé, not to a business restarting.

**What that means in practice:** you would spend four years building a brand you cannot
stop a competitor from copying. Any rival could open "Global Shoppers" next door and you
would have little to enforce.

### 4. You will never rank for it

It is a generic two-word English phrase. Organic search is unwinnable, and every paid
rupee competes against the literal phrase in a global auction. **For a company with one
marketing person, an unwinnable search term is a permanent tax.**

## gcom — the better of the two new names, with one hard condition

### What is genuinely good

- **"Global Commerce" is directionally neutral.** Commerce flows both ways. For a
  two-way import-export business this is exactly right, and it is the single biggest
  advantage over Global Shopper.
- **Four letters.** Short names read as established. Easy to say once on a phone call
  in Hyderabad or Shenzhen and have it land.
- **Corporate, not consumer.** No B2C baggage to fight.
- **Registrable.** As a coined contraction it is not descriptive the way "Global
  Shopper" is, so Section 9 is not a wall.
- **You have used it before**, so some internal familiarity exists.

### What is genuinely bad — and the first one is serious

**1. You own gcom.in, not gcom.com. For a company whose stated ambition is *global*
business, that is a contradiction sitting in the address bar.**

- **gcom.com is held by GCOM Software LLC**, a US technology consulting firm serving
  government clients. `FACT` They are not going to sell cheaply, if at all.
- **.com carries a measured trust and traffic advantage** — one study puts click-through
  **39% higher** on .com than newer extensions, and B2B buyers in conservative
  industries specifically prefer .com. `CLAIM` — single study, treat directionally
- **A .in address tells a foreign buyer "Indian company, Indian market."** That is fine
  when you are exporting Indian goods. **It works against you when you are asking a
  Chinese supplier or a foreign buyer to see you as a global trading house.**
- **Typo leakage is permanent.** Every person who types gcom.com — the default guess —
  lands on a US IT contractor. You will pay for that in lost enquiries forever.

**2. "gcom" is one of the most generic constructions available.** A letter plus "com."
Glocom, GCOM Software, and many others occupy the space. Legally registrable is not the
same as distinctive in the market. **You would be the fourth gcom, not the first.**

**3. It carries no equity.** Used two years ago and dropped. That is a false start, not
heritage. Do not count it as a head start.

**4. The name has no story.** "Global Commerce" describes a *category*, not a *promise*.
It says what you do. It does not say why anyone should pick you. Compare to what Befach
already has.

### The hard condition

**Do not launch gcom on gcom.in alone.** Fix the domain first, in this order:

1. **Try to buy gcom.com.** Get a price. It may be out of reach, but a broker enquiry
   costs nothing and you need the number to decide.
2. **If not: buy a proper global alternative** — `gcom.trade` reads extremely well for
   this business and is likely available and cheap. `gcomglobal.com` or `gcom.co` also
   work. **Any of these beats .in for a global positioning.**
3. **Keep gcom.in as the India-facing redirect.** It is genuinely useful for Indian
   buyers and for export credibility when selling Indian goods out.

**If you cannot secure a credible global domain, gcom's main advantage over Befach
disappears** — because at that point you have swapped a registered mark on a .com for an
unregistered mark on a .in.

## Befach — the asset you already own, and the architecture question

### What you actually have here, valued honestly

| Asset | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Registered trademark in India** | Real, enforceable legal property. The other two names are **12–18 months away uncontested, 24–36 months if opposed** `FACT` |
| **befach.com owned** | The .com you would have to buy for gcom, you already hold here |
| **8 years of use, ₹13.5 Cr traded under it** | Acquired distinctiveness is a fact, not an aspiration |
| **~100 import clients know the name** | Small, but it is the warm list your GTM depends on |
| **Live catalogue at befach.com** | Whatever SEO, links and API integrations exist are attached to this domain |
| **Coined word** | Coined marks are the **strongest** trademark class — Kodak, Xerox, Zomato. Descriptive marks are the weakest. |

### What is genuinely wrong with it

- **The phone test fails.** BEE-fatch? Beh-FAK? A name your one salesperson has to spell
  on every call is a small tax paid thousands of times. **This is the strongest argument
  against Befach and it should not be waved away.**
- **The acronym is invisible.** Nobody decodes Better-Faster-Cheaper from "Befach"
  unaided. An asset you must explain is weak — *until you explain it consistently*,
  which has never been done.
- **Hard for a Chinese supplier to say.** Real friction in your main sourcing market.
- **It carries the history of a stalled business.** ~100 clients, one repeat buyer. Some
  of those people associate the name with a company that did not deliver.

### The architecture point — worth one re-look before you decide

Your chosen structure is: **Befach 4x = parent, holding D'Cal and Rice. Imports gets a
new name.**

**Look at what that does to the meaning.**

**Befach means Better, Faster, Cheaper. That is a trade-services promise.** It describes
making importing better, faster and cheaper. It does **not** describe a diabetic rice or
a water softener.

Your entire import USP — **fixed landed price, customs handled, faster clearance,
cheaper than the trader's markup** — *is literally better, faster, cheaper.*
**The name already contains the strategy of the import business.**

The proposed architecture takes the name whose meaning is about trade and attaches it to
two consumer products, then gives the trade business a name with no story.

> **The inverse is worth considering: keep Befach as the trade brand, and let D'Cal and
> Rice keep their own names as product brands.** D'Cal and Befach Rice already stand
> alone in their markets and do not need the parent's name to sell.

This is your call, not mine — but the reasoning should be on the table before you spend
money and 18 months registering something new.

## The timing argument — this cuts in favour of deciding now

**A rename is cheapest at ₹0.5 crore of import revenue and one salesperson.**

The costs of renaming — new trademark filing, domain purchase, IEC and AD-code
paperwork, website, collateral, re-educating existing clients — are roughly fixed. They
do not grow much with revenue. **But what you throw away grows every year.**

**So: if you are going to change the name, now is the cheapest moment you will ever
have. Do not defer this to ₹50 crore.** That is a real argument for acting, not waiting.

The counter, stated fairly: **your own diagnosis of the last eight years was portfolio
architecture, not branding.** One product in each of two unrelated categories with no
shared customer. **A new name does not fix that.** If the rename becomes the project
instead of a two-week decision, it is displacement activity — and you have one
salesperson and three interns who cannot afford the distraction.

## Recommendation

**Between your two new options: gcom, clearly. Global Shopper should be dropped today.**

**But before committing to gcom, do these three things — they take a week and they
decide it:**

1. **Get a price on gcom.com.** Use a domain broker. If it is affordable, gcom becomes a
   strong, clean answer and the decision is easy.
2. **Run a trademark search on GCOM in Class 35 (trading, import-export agency
   services) and Class 39 (freight, logistics)** on the Indian TM registry. "gcom" is a
   crowded string — confirm it is clear in your classes before spending anything.
3. **Do the phone test on both names, for real.** Have your one import salesperson say
   each name to twenty prospects and count how many spell it correctly without help.
   **That single number settles the Befach-versus-gcom question better than any
   argument in this note.**

**And put the architecture question back on the table once.** Befach = Better, Faster,
Cheaper is the best positioning line an import business could ask for, you own the
trademark and the .com, and it is currently pointed at rice.

---

## What would change this recommendation

- **gcom.com turns out to be buyable at a sane price** → gcom becomes the clear answer
- **The trademark search shows GCOM blocked in Class 35 or 39** → gcom is dead, and the
  decision collapses back to Befach
- **The phone test shows Befach spelled correctly by most prospects** → the single
  biggest objection to Befach disappears
- **You decide to export more than you import** → an India-flagged identity becomes an
  asset rather than a limit, and gcom.in stops being a liability
