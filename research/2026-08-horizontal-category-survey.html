Where the pain is

Twenty-three B2B categories, mapped for market size, incumbent crowding, customer pain and execution difficulty — and the decision you just made that reopened all of them.

Bottom line

Electricals and fast-moving electrical goods is the standout. Auto aftermarket is second. Artificial jewellery is the cheapest place to start.

Electricals is a ₹8.7 lakh crore market growing at 10.7%, where industry research states plainly that "the supply chain for fast-moving electrical goods is quite fragmented, which makes it difficult to ensure availability of the products in the market in the desired quality." Fact That is a pain statement, not a market description — and no funded player has taken the category.

The bigger news: by making imports secondary you have deleted the constraint that was killing every other option.

What your pivot just unlocked

Every category screen I have given you so far was built on the assumption that you are the importer. That assumption carried a brutal constraint: India has over 400 product categories under mandatory BIS certification, and anything under a Quality Control Order cannot be imported without approval, verified at the port. Fact It closed electronics, toys, footwear, lighting, appliances and furniture outright.

The unlock

When you source domestically, the BIS certificate is the manufacturer's obligation, not yours. A distributor of Indian-made goods is not an importer and does not carry import compliance risk on those goods.

By demoting imports to secondary, you have removed the single biggest barrier in every previous screen. Electricals, lighting, appliances, toys and footwear all come back onto the table — provided you buy them from Indian manufacturers rather than shipping them in.

This is the most consequential decision you have made in this whole conversation, and it was almost a side note in how you described it.

It also reframes what imports are for. Not the business — the margin layer. You distribute domestic brands to win the customer, then import and private-label the highest-repeat SKUs once you know what moves. Sourcing stays a weapon and stops being the product.

Why pharmacy scores 18 despite being the biggest opportunity on the list

800,000+ retail pharmacies and 80,000+ distributors is one of the most fragmented, highest-repeat structures in India. Fact It should be perfect. It is not, for one reason that has nothing to do with logistics:

In many states, launching a product requires paying commissions to chemist associations to obtain a mandatory no-objection certificate for market availability. Fact

That is an organised trade body with the power to gate distribution. Add a drug licence, expiry-dated stock, and Saveo and Retailio already funded and fighting. The pain is real and you are not the one who can relieve it. Avoid.

The top three, in detail

1 — Electricals and fast-moving electrical goods

Size: the electricals sector was forecast to reach ₹8,732 billion in 2025 growing at 10.7% CAGR, with the electrical equipment market adding $110.7 Bn between 2026 and 2030 at 15.9%. Fact That is an order of magnitude above anything else on the list except grocery and pharmacy, both of which are closed to you.

The pain is documented, not inferred. Industry research states the FMEG supply chain is fragmented in a way that makes it hard to ensure availability of the right products at the right quality. Availability and authenticity are exactly the two things B2B buyers pay for.

Why it is open: SwitchBazaar and Industricals exist but neither has scale or serious funding. For a market this size, that is a genuine anomaly — and worth understanding before you commit, because it may be an anomaly with a reason.

Execution is easier than auto. Wires, switches, MCBs, fans, lights, fittings and accessories — no vehicle fitment matrix, no expiry, no cold chain, no size-colour explosion. Heavy, but not fragile.

Fit to you: domestic supply from Indian manufacturers means no import compliance. Then the private-label layer on high-repeat accessories uses your China capability exactly as a weapon should be used.

2 — Auto parts aftermarket

₹99,948 Cr in FY25, extreme fragmentation, unorganised distribution, and a defining counterfeit problem that a trusted supplier can convert into a value proposition. Fact Boodmo is the only notable player, which is remarkable for the size.

The reason it drops to second is execution: the make/model/year/variant fitment matrix is years of data work and the graveyard of this category. The wedge — entering on fitment-agnostic consumables — still stands, but it is a harder first year than electricals.

3 — Artificial jewellery

~₹42,000 Cr growing 10%, entirely open, no regulatory surface, and the single best fit for your China sourcing. Fact Simplest logistics of the three: light, non-fragile, no expiry, no certification.

It scores third only because the TAM caps out below an IPO-scale story and because design risk sits with whoever holds stock. If capital is tight, this is where you start — it is the cheapest possible test of whether you can run a B2B distribution business at all.

The question I cannot answer from a desk

Electricals is a ₹8.7 lakh crore market with no funded B2B platform. Either that is the largest unclaimed opportunity in Indian B2B commerce, or there is a reason nobody has taken it and I have not found it.

Candidate reasons worth testing before you commit: brand territory lock-ins may be tighter than they appear; credit terms may be non-negotiable; counterfeit and BIS liability on electrical goods carries safety consequences that a distributor cannot insure against; and contractors may buy on project credit in a way that makes working capital impossible.

Thirty conversations in the Hyderabad electrical market answers this in a week. Do not commit capital before you have had them.

Recommendation

  1. Run the field test on electricals first — Hyderabad's electrical market, thirty conversations, the four questions from the earlier memo. You are testing one thing above all: why has nobody done this?
  2. Auto aftermarket second, same test, same week if you have the people.
  3. Artificial jewellery as the low-capital fallback if the answer to either is "capital requirement is beyond us."
  4. Drop pharmacy, grocery, restaurant supplies, hospital supplies, packaging, agri and industrial MRO permanently. All are large; all have funded incumbents ahead of you; none can be won from a standing start in Hyderabad in 2026.
  5. Keep imports as the private-label layer, deployed once a category is proven — never as the customer proposition.

One consistency check worth stating plainly: the model that comes out of this survey is a domestic-first vertical distributor with an import-powered private-label layer. That is Infra.Market's structure — ₹18,472 Cr of revenue, IPO filed — applied to a different category. It is a proven shape in India, it uses all four of your stated fortes, and it needs no R&D or manufacturing.
